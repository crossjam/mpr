[NetNewsWire] officially [turned 23][netnewswire-birthday] last week!
🎉 💥 🎂

I first [mentioned NetNewsWire in a post][netnewswire-first-post] back
on February 4th of 2003. _Yes, really!!_. Then followed it up [in
another post][netnewswire-post] a few days later:

> Brent Simmons describes how he gets one click subscription from a
> Web page to NetNewsWire. 
>
> Memo to self: Can Chimera do that?

Very much back in my formative era of blogging. Looks like that was
within the first 50 posts. Don’t actually remember what Chimera was.

I’m pretty confident I was using the 1.0 precursor, either NetNewsWire
(paid) or more likely NetNewsWire Lite, well in advance of the 1.0
release. I’ve been at least an intermittent user of NNW over the years
except for a gap when [it wasn’t owned][netnewswire-wikipedia] by
[Brent Simmons][brent-simmons] and development had stalled a bit,
especially on iOS. Also, [Google Reader][google-reader] was still on
the scene and quite good, so it might have become my main feed
reader. Gettin’ hazy on the details but there’s a few posts on this
here blog about the situation.

Today, I use the iOS version of NetNewsWire, primarily synced with
[Feedbin](https://feedbin.com), every single day. My set of daily apps
isn’t all that small, banking and news apps help up the count, but
it’s one of the few where I’d be really put out if I had to give it
up. Feed reading, an essential personal activity, is effectively
quarantined to my phone and not allowed on the desktop anymore. NNW is
the nexus of my feed reading.

Here’s to another two decades of the gift that is NetNewsWire!
